MANEKA GANDHI V. UNION OF INDIA (1978) 1 SCC 248
SUPREME COURT OF INDIA
Case No: Writ Petition (Civil) No. 231 of 1977
Bench: 7-Judge Constitution Bench

SUMMARY & RATIO DECIDENDI:
The Supreme Court expanded the scope of Article 21 of the Constitution of India. It ruled that "procedure established by law" must be just, fair, and reasonable, and not arbitrary, fanciful, or oppressive.

KEY PRINCIPLES:
1. Procedural Due Process: Any law impinging upon personal liberty under Article 21 must pass the test of reasonableness under Articles 14 and 19.
2. Right to Travel Abroad: Personal liberty includes the right to travel abroad.
3. Principles of Natural Justice: Right to audi alteram partem (right to be heard) is an intrinsic component of Article 21.

STATUTORY SECTIONS REFERENCED:
- Passport Act, 1967 (Section 10(3)(c))
- Article 21, Article 14, Article 19 of the Constitution of India
