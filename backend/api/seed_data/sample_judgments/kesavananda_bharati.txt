KESAVANANDA BHARATI V. STATE OF KERALA (1973) 4 SCC 225
SUPREME COURT OF INDIA
Case No: Writ Petition (Civil) No. 135 of 1970
Bench: 13-Judge Constitution Bench

SUMMARY & RATIO DECIDENDI:
The Supreme Court of India held that while Parliament has wide power under Article 368 to amend any part of the Constitution of India, it cannot amend or alter the "Basic Structure" or essential features of the Constitution.

KEY PRINCIPLES:
1. Basic Structure Doctrine: Parliamentary power to amend the Constitution is subject to inherent limitations.
2. Judicial Review: Supremacy of the Constitution, Rule of Law, Separation of Powers, Secularism, and Federalism constitute core components of the Basic Structure.
3. Fundamental Rights vs Directive Principles: Harmony between Part III (Fundamental Rights) and Part IV (Directive Principles) must be preserved.

STATUTORY SECTIONS REFERENCED:
- Article 368 of the Constitution of India (Power to Amend)
- Article 13 of the Constitution of India (Laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights)
- 24th, 25th, and 29th Constitutional Amendment Acts
